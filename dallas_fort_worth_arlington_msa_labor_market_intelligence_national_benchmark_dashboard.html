EXECUTIVE INTELLIGENCE BRIEF • DALLAS–FORT WORTH–ARLINGTON MSA

DFW Ranks #4 Nationally in Labor Force (4.61M) and Drives 80.9% of Texas’s Total Annual Workforce Expansion

In September 2026(p), the Dallas–Fort Worth–Arlington MSA expanded its Civilian Labor Force to 4,611,425 (+13,661 / +0.30% YoY), accounting for 80.9% of Texas’s statewide net labor force growth (+16,894). Employed residents rose +16,191 (+0.37% YoY) while the unemployment rate dropped -0.1 pp YoY and -0.3 pp MoM to 4.1%—outperforming Texas statewide (4.3%) as well as peer mega-metros including New York (4.5%), Los Angeles (4.4%), Chicago (4.4%), and Houston (4.6%).

1. Talent Acquisition & Retention

With DFW unemployment tightening to 4.1% (-12,727 unemployed MoM) while coastal metros contract (NYC -0.08% YoY, Chicago -1.83% YoY, SF -1.91% YoY), corporate relocation and mid-career recruitment campaigns from high-cost metros offer peak ROI.

2. Sector Reallocation & Upskilling

Labor supply growth (+0.30% YoY) is lagging employment absorption (+0.37% YoY). Workforce boards should accelerate rapid credentialing in healthcare, advanced manufacturing, and AI-augmented business services to prevent wage-push bottlenecks.

3. Sub-Regional Texas Spillover

DFW generates 80.9% of Texas’s net CLF gain while border metros face elevated slack (Eagle Pass 6.2%, Brownsville 5.9%, McAllen 5.6%). Remote-hybrid talent pipelines and corridor infrastructure link DFW demand to statewide labor reserves.
